We are building an open-source, browser-based, drill-design tool for marching bands and drum corps, called "Freeform". This tool would be accompanied by a phone/tablet (ios/android) app that allows individual performers to see the drill movements from the director's perspective and from their personal perspective. Show creators/drill writers must be able to place performers on a football field (default), basketball court, or other custom surface; define sets; animate transitions across counts; and export drill charts and coordinate sheets as PDFs and to the performer app. A football field must be configured to accept high-school, college/drum corps, or NFL hash marks. For American football fields, Side 1 is defined as the left side of the 50 yard line from the perspective of the director. Side 2 is defined as the right side of the 50 yard line from the perspective of the director. The basic step size should be 8-to-5 (8 steps to 5 yards).

Drill writers must be able to place performers (individually or in groups) on a marching surface (as dots, letters, or icons) into different formations, called "sets" or "pages." Transitions between sets would take a drill-writer-defined number of counts. Drill-writers will be able to include text and make some mark-up on individual pages, e.g., to give performers the number of counts in a movement, measure numbers, body position, horn angle, arrows for direction of movement on a follow-the-leader type move, etc. Drill-writers should be able to scrub and play individual sets, sub-collections of sets, or the whole collection of sets, called a "show," as an animated timeline. Individual performers should be able to scrub and play individual sets, a sub-collection of sets, or the entire show as an animated timeline from the director's perspective and from their own perspective.

Drill-writers should also be able to sync music files (mp3, midi, etc.) to the show so that they, ensemble directors, and performers and see a more complete show in digital form so that they can more easily translate that digital show to real 3-D space.

Importantly, drill-writers will need to be able to place performers into lines, arcs, curviliniear, free, and other shapes easily. If the distance between two sets for a given performer is larger than a 6-to-5 (6 steps to 5 yards), based on the number of counts between those two sets, the drill-writer should be notified in some way. Pyware handles this notification by turning the performer's path between sets red and showing the stepsize in red text in the timeline. As a rule of thumb, the largest step size is a 4-to-5 (4 steps to 5 yards). Since a performer may have the same drill coordinate for two or more consecutive sets, there is no smallest step size. Drill writers should have the ability to set certain transitions between sets for an arbitrary number of performers as "float" (default) or "follow-the-leader" or "FTL." If a transition is set to FTL, the  drill writer should be able to specify the "line leader" and the specify the order of the selected performers. In an FTL movement, performers move in order toward the leader through the field positions of the preceeding performers throughout the movement.

Drill writers and performers should be able to play back a given movement/transition-between-sets on a count by count basis, pausing on any count needed to gain clarity for the movement.

The ideas for these tools is based on Pyware (https://www.pyware.com/) and the Ultimate Drill Book (UDB) App (https://www.ultimatedrillbook.com/udbapp/). The goal for this project is to remove Pyware's/UDB's licensing cost as a barrier for school and community programs.
